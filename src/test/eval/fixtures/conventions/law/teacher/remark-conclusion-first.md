# Torts and Contracts — Lecturer's Remarks for the 2025 Exams
From the course lecturer, to the teaching assistants and to students preparing for the 2025 exams.

Having read the 2023 and 2024 answer sheets, I want to change one thing about how answers are written, starting with the 2025 exams and the model solutions that accompany them.

From now on: put the conclusion FIRST, in the opening paragraph, then the analysis. Open every problem-question answer by stating whether the claim succeeds or fails and on what basis, in two or three sentences. After that, set out the issue, the rule and the application in support of the conclusion already given. Do not add a closing section that repeats the result; the answer ends when the analysis ends.

The reason is practical. An examiner who reads sixty scripts wants to know immediately where the examinee has landed, and can then judge whether the reasoning that follows supports it. The same discipline serves practitioners: a client or a judge reads the outcome first.

A few reminders that carry over from previous years:

- The remark applies to problem questions. Short definition questions may be answered in the order that is natural to them.
- Marks for the conclusion are given for its consistency with the analysis.
- Model solutions for 2025 will follow this order and will be published after each exam.
