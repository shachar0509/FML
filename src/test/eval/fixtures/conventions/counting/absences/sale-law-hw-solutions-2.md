# Commercial Law — Homework 3, Model Answers (Part 2)

## Question 1 (20 points)
Tal sold 200 jars of honey to a shop for 4,000 shekels. The shop took delivery and then refused to pay. What may Tal do?

**Issue.** What remedies a seller has when the buyer, having taken the goods, does not pay.

**Rule.** According to the Sale Law, 1968, s. 20 the buyer must pay the price on delivery unless agreed otherwise, and the seller may sue for the price once the buyer has received the goods. Interest accrues on the unpaid sum from the day payment was due.

**Application.** There was no agreement postponing payment, so the 4,000 shekels were due on delivery under s. 20. The shop took the honey, so Tal does not need to cancel the sale or recover the goods; a claim for the price is available. The refusal was without reason.

**Conclusion.** Tal may sue for the 4,000 shekels together with interest from the delivery date, as provided in s. 20.

## Question 2 (20 points)
Rina's contract with a supplier says "delivery at the seller's warehouse". A fire destroyed the goods in the warehouse after the supplier had prepared them but before Rina collected them. Who bears the loss?

**Issue.** Who carries the risk of loss of goods destroyed before collection.

**Rule.** The Sale Law, 1968, s. 22 provides that the risk of accidental loss passes to the buyer when the goods are delivered, and where the buyer is bound to collect them, from the moment they are placed at the buyer's disposal and the buyer has been told so.

**Application.** The goods were prepared, but the facts do not say Rina was told they were at her disposal. Without that notice the risk had not passed under s. 22, and the fire occurred while the goods were still in the supplier's hands.

**Conclusion.** The supplier bears the loss under s. 22 and must supply the goods again or return any money paid.

## Question 3 (20 points)
A supplier's standard form allows him to cancel any order at any time without reason, while the buyer may cancel only against a fee of half the price. A buyer challenges this clause. Is it enforceable?

**Issue.** Whether a one-sided cancellation clause in a standard form is enforceable.

**Rule.** The Sale Law is the background statute for the sale in this case, but the clause is examined under the general duty of good faith in performing and enforcing contracts and under the case law on standard forms, which asks whether a term gives the drafter an advantage that has no reasonable justification.

**Application.** The supplier can cancel freely and the buyer cannot, so the clause is unbalanced. The supplier gave no commercial reason such as a risk that would justify the difference, and a buyer has no real chance to negotiate a printed form. The case law treats such a gap as grounds to void the unfair term.

**Conclusion.** The clause is likely to be struck down as unfair, while the rest of the contract stays in force.

## Question 4 (10 points)
Explain what is meant by "title" in a sale of goods.

**Issue.** The meaning of the term title.

**Rule.** Title is the ownership of the goods, the right to hold and dispose of them against everyone.

**Application.** In a sale the seller transfers title to the buyer. The buyer who gets title may resell or destroy the goods, and the seller no longer may. Title is separate from possession: a buyer may hold goods without title, as a hirer does.

**Conclusion.** Title means ownership, and a sale is the transfer of title for a price.
